## Bottom line

- **20 companies passed my minimum direct-factory standard: 5 with Strong evidence and 15 with Moderate evidence.** None of them is "safe" or "fraud-proof". Every one still needs the pre-payment checks in Section G.
- **Highest confidence (Strong):** #1 Beijing Chengdong (CDPH), #2 Ningbo Deepblue Smarthouse, #3 Hebei Weizhengheng (WZH), #4 Zhejiang Putian (PTH), #5 Zhuhai Remac Space (Midea group).
- **Named third-party on-site verification:** #7 Mars Cabin (TÜV Rheinland), #8 Moneybox (TÜV Rheinland), #9 The Box (Intertek), #17 Tianjin Yuantai (Alibaba on-site check).
- **For the US market:** only **#2 Deepblue** has a confirmed US code-body document: ICC-ES report ESR-5348. It covers the steel *framing members*, not a whole house. **#1 CDPH** has a confirmed **Canadian** welding certification (CWB). **I found no factory with HUD, state-modular-program or RVIA approval.** Plan for US engineering and local approval yourself (Section I).
- **Removed:** 28 other companies. 21 were excluded as traders, too small, too new, or showing warning signs, and 7 near-misses are listed as alternates (Section F).

## How this research was done (read first)

1. **Your Chrome browser could not be used.** This research ran in a cloud environment with no connection to your computer, and the environment's network policy **blocked direct access to alibaba.com**.
2. **Alibaba data comes from search-engine-indexed copies of each supplier's own Alibaba store and company-profile pages** (`xxx.en.alibaba.com`). These are the same pages you will see, but the copies can be weeks or months old. I ignored Alibaba's auto-generated "supplier guide" and "showroom" SEO pages for facts, because they mix up companies. Where a figure only came from one of those pages, it is labelled.
3. **Independent cross-checks:** the ICC-ES report directory, the CWB Group certified-company directory, trade directories (Tradekey, Tradewheel, B2USA, HKTDC), trade-show exhibitor lists (IBS 2025, Sydney Build Expo), trade press, and the Indian consulates' trade-dispute pages.
4. **About 48 companies were screened.** Each was researched individually: legal name, label (custom manufacturer / multispecialty supplier / manufacturer & trading), plant size, lines, staff, certificates, verifier, products, prices and inconsistencies.
5. **Not visible in the indexed data, so check it live:** the "X yrs" badge, the Verified badge and verifier name, Trade Assurance, transaction history and recent reviews. Alibaba hides phone/WhatsApp/WeChat behind login. Only contact details that appeared publicly are listed, with their source.
6. Wording used: **"Not independently verified from available information."** means I could not confirm that data point. **"Not publicly verified"** means no contact detail was found.

**Evidence scale:** *Strong* = Alibaba manufacturer label + detailed plant data + at least one independent corroboration. *Moderate* = manufacturer label + specific plant data or a named third-party on-site verification, but gaps or no independent corroboration. *Insufficient* = excluded.
**Risk scale:** *Low / Some concerns / Significant concerns.* This is based only on warning signs visible in public data. Companies with significant concerns were removed.

## E. Safe payment procedure (step by step)

1. **Inquiry.** Contact 5–8 factories from this list through Alibaba chat using the Section C message. Keep all conversations inside Alibaba.
2. **Quotation.** Compare itemised EXW and FOB quotes like-for-like on steel thickness, panel type and thickness, electrical spec and inclusions. Be suspicious of any quote far below the others.
3. **Verification.** Run the Section G checklist: business licence, USCC lookup, live video factory walk-through, certificate number checks, and (for large orders) a third-party factory audit (typically a few hundred US dollars from QIMA, SGS, BV, Intertek or TÜV).
4. **Sample or prototype.** Order one unit or a panel/frame sample where practical, or visit a finished unit at the factory by video or in person. Test it, and have a US engineer review the drawings before any bulk order.
5. **Contract and PI.** Put every specification, dimension, material, accessory, packaging, quality standard, inspection right, delivery date, Incoterm, warranty and penalty in writing. The company name on the PI, the contract and the bank account must all match the verified business licence.
6. **Deposit (typically 30%).** Pay **only through Alibaba Trade Assurance** where available, or by wire to a **corporate** account in the exact company name. **Never pay a personal account**, and never pay to "new" bank details received by email.
7. **During-production inspection.** At about 30–50% completion, a third-party inspector checks steel, welding, galvanizing, panels and wiring against the contract.
8. **Pre-shipment inspection (PSI).** At 100% completion, before the balance is paid: full inspection against the spec sheet, including function tests of expansion/folding, water spray test, electrical tests and photos of every unit.
9. **Balance payment (typically 70%).** Pay only after the PSI report passes, through Trade Assurance or to the same verified corporate account. For large orders, consider paying the balance against a copy of the bill of lading, or using a documentary letter of credit that requires the inspection certificate.
10. **Loading and shipment.** For large orders, have the inspector supervise container loading (count, lashing, protection). Get the bill of lading and arrange your own marine cargo insurance. Under CIF the seller only has to buy minimal cover, so buy "all-risks" (ICC A) insurance yourself. Your customs broker handles import duties, entry and delivery.

## I. Selling in the United States (and Canada, UK/EU, Australia)

**CE, ISO, SGS or Alibaba verification does not mean a house is legal to sell, place or occupy in the United States.** In the US the house must fit a recognised category, and each has its own approval route:

| US category | Rules | What a Chinese factory would need | Status in this research |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manufactured home** (on permanent chassis) | HUD Code, 24 CFR 3280. Built in a HUD-approved plant, with a HUD label from a third-party inspection agency | HUD plant approval (design approval + in-plant inspection) | **None found** for any of the 20 |
| **Modular home** (on a permanent foundation) | IRC/IBC as adopted locally; most states run a factory-built / industrialized housing program with third-party plan approval, in-plant inspection and a state insignia | State-approved plans and inspection agency, or a US engineer/inspector arrangement | **None found.** Ask each factory |
| **Tiny house on wheels / park model** | Usually treated as an RV (NFPA 1192) or park model RV (ANSI A119.5); RVIA certification commonly required by parks and lenders | RVIA certification or a US RV-maker partner | **None found** |
| **Accessory dwelling / backyard cabin** | Local permit under the IRC (some places adopt the IRC tiny-house appendix), PE-stamped plans, local inspection | Engineering drawings a US PE can stamp; listed components | Depends on documents. #2 Deepblue is closest (ICC-ES-evaluated framing) |
| **Container-based building** | IBC 2021 Section 3115 and ICC G5 guideline (repurposed ISO shipping containers) | Note: most "expandable/folding container houses" are **not** ISO containers but light-steel modules, so they need their own engineering | – |

**Key US technical points**
- **Electrical:** must meet the NEC (NFPA 70). Inspectors expect UL- or ETL-listed panels, breakers, wiring and devices. Chinese standard wiring normally has to be replaced. Ask for a "US version" with listed components.
- **Plumbing and fuel gas:** IPC/UPC with listed fixtures (cUPC/NSF).
- **Structure:** wind (ASCE 7 design speeds are often about 100–115 mph inland and much higher on hurricane coasts), snow and seismic loads must be engineered for **your customer's site**. Budget for a US-licensed PE to review and stamp drawings and design the foundation and anchorage.
- **Energy:** IECC or state energy code insulation levels.
- **Fire:** foam-plastic insulation (EPS/PU) generally needs a thermal barrier such as ½" gypsum board on the interior.
- **Composite wood** (plywood, MDF, particleboard) in imported finished goods must comply with **EPA TSCA Title VI** formaldehyde rules (certified and labelled).
- **Import duties:** prefabricated buildings fall under HTS heading 9406. Chinese-origin goods have been subject to Section 301 and other additional tariffs that changed repeatedly in 2025–2026. **Get the current classification and duty rate from a licensed US customs broker before quoting customers.**
- **Business protection:** product-liability insurance, clear customer contracts (who handles permits, foundation and installation), and honest marketing ("permit-ready" only if documents exist).

- **Ningbo Deepblue Smarthouse Co., Ltd.** – Strongest US relevance found: ICC-ES ESR-5348 (IBC), IBS 2025 exhibitor. Still ask for state-licensed PE-stamped drawings and UL-listed electrical components.

**Canada:** factory-built houses are usually certified to **CSA A277** (third-party factory certification) and the National/provincial building code. Mobile homes follow the **CSA Z240 MH** series. Electrical components need **CSA/cUL/cETL** marks. Steel welding firms are certified under **CSA W47.1** through CWB: #1 CDPH is confirmed in the CWB directory, and #4 Putian claims CWB (unconfirmed).

**United Kingdom:** "mobile homes / park homes" must meet the size limits in the Caravan Sites Acts. Residential park homes are commonly built to **BS 3632**. Permanent modular homes need **Building Regulations** approval (fire safety under Approved Document B is critical for foam-cored panels), and construction products need **UKCA/CE** marking.

**European Union:** construction products need **CE marking under the Construction Products Regulation**. Structural steel components need **EN 1090** (#2 Deepblue claims it), designed to the **Eurocodes**. Each country's building code governs placement and occupancy.

**Australia:** the **National Construction Code** applies, with wind classification to **AS 4055 / AS/NZS 1170.2**. Electrical work must meet **AS/NZS 3000** with RCM-approved equipment (SAA certificates), and plumbing needs **WaterMark** products (#3 WZH claims SAA and WaterMark). **Asbestos is a prohibited import.** Insist on NATA-accredited testing (e.g. **AS 4964**; #12 Heshi lists a test report) before shipping, as Australian Border Force can seize goods.
